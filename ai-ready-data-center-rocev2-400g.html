AI-Ready Data Centers — RoCEv2, Lossless Ethernet & 400G GPU Fabrics

GPU clusters don't fail like web farms. One paused queue, one PFC storm, one ECMP hash collision — and a million-dollar training run slows to a crawl. Designing for AI means designing for the tail, not the average.

The workload is the spec

AI training traffic is unlike anything classic DC design assumed. It is synchronized, bursty, elephant-flow east-west traffic: thousands of GPUs exchanging gradients in collective operations where the slowest path defines the speed of the whole job. A 0.1% packet loss that no web app would notice can cut training throughput dramatically, because RoCEv2's loss recovery is brutal compared to TCP.

Lossless Ethernet, done carefully

RoCEv2 needs a lossless fabric, and that means PFC + ECN tuned together, not just enabled:

  • ECN does the work, PFC is the safety net. Set WRED/ECN thresholds low enough that DCQCN reacts before queues fill; PFC should fire rarely. If you see steady PFC pause counters, your ECN thresholds are wrong.
  • Watch for PFC storms and deadlocks. Priority pause propagates upstream. Headroom buffers must be sized for cable length and port speed, and PFC watchdogs are mandatory.
  • Separate traffic classes. RDMA in its own queue, CNPs prioritized, storage and management isolated. One misclassified flow can poison the lossless class.

Topology: rail-optimized vs. classic Clos

For GPU pods, rail-optimized designs — where GPU N of every server connects to leaf N — keep collective traffic a single hop for intra-rail communication and dramatically reduce spine load for NCCL's ring and tree algorithms. The frontend network (storage via NVMe-oF, orchestration, user access) stays a conventional EVPN/VXLAN fabric; the backend GPU fabric is its own world with its own failure domain.

400G and the optics problem

At 400G the design conversation becomes a physical-layer conversation: power per port, optics cost dominating switch cost, FEC latency, and DR4/FR4 reach planning. Budget optics first, switches second — it's routinely 60%+ of fabric cost.

From my ACI Multi-Pod migration days, the lesson that carries over: the fabric you can observe is the fabric you can scale. Streaming telemetry on queue depth, ECN marks, and PFC counters per port is not optional in an AI fabric — it's how you find the one bad cable killing your job.

The checklist

Buffer-monitor before you scale. Validate with real collectives (NCCL tests, not iperf). Keep the GPU fabric flat, fast, and boring. And treat every firmware/NOS upgrade as a science experiment — the DCQCN behavior you tuned is implementation-specific.
